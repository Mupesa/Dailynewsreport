## 🧠 The Most Interesting Person in the Room: Psychology & Human Quirks
- **The Mind-Expanding Fact**: **The "IKEA Effect."** Research shows that humans disproportionately value objects they helped create. When we invest labor into a product—whether it’s assembling furniture or writing a report—we develop an irrational attachment to it, valuing it significantly higher than its market utility. We don't just love what we build; we love it *because* we built it.
- **The Dinner-Table Conversation Starter**: "Have you ever noticed that people are fiercely defensive of their own bad ideas? It’s called the IKEA Effect. We literally fall in love with our own labor. It’s why companies fail to pivot; the founders are so attached to the 'furniture' they built that they can’t see it’s wobbly."
- **The Mental Model / Razor**: **The "Zero-Base Ownership" Razor.** When evaluating your own projects or strategies, ask: "If I didn't build this, would I buy it today?" If the answer is no, you are trapped by the IKEA Effect. Detach the effort from the outcome.
