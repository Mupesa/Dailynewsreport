## 🧠 The Most Interesting Person in the Room: Psychology & Human Quirks
- **The Phenomenon: The "IKEA Effect."** Humans place a disproportionately high value on products they partially created themselves, even if the result is objectively worse than a store-bought alternative. It is a cognitive bias where labor leads to love; the more effort you invest in a task, the more you defend its quality.
- **The Conversation Starter:** "Have you ever noticed that people are fiercely defensive of their own half-baked ideas or DIY projects? It’s called the IKEA Effect—we’re psychologically hardwired to overvalue our own labor. It explains why companies that let customers customize their products (like Nike or software platforms) see higher loyalty; they aren't just selling a product, they’re selling the user’s sense of authorship."
- **The Mental Model: The "Sunk Effort" Awareness.** Recognize that your attachment to a project often stems from your *input*, not the *output's* actual value. When you find yourself defending a failing strategy, ask: "If I hadn't built this from scratch, would I be this committed to it?" If the answer is no, cut your losses.
