- **The Mind-Expanding Fact**: **The "Tulipomania" Misconception.** History books often cite the 17th-century Dutch tulip crash as the ultimate irrational market bubble. In reality, modern economic historians (like Anne Goldgar) have found little evidence of a widespread economic collapse. The "mania" was largely a morality play—a piece of anti-capitalist propaganda written by Calvinist writers to warn against the dangers of greed and social mobility. The "crash" was a localized dispute among a small group of wealthy speculators, not a national bankruptcy. 
- **The Dinner-Table Conversation Starter**: "Everyone loves to talk about market bubbles like they’re objective facts, but have you ever noticed that most famous 'crashes'—like the Tulip mania—are actually just morality tales invented by the winners to make us fear taking risks? We’re often trading based on historical myths rather than actual economic data."
- **The Mental Model**: **The Narrative Discount.** Before accepting a "lesson" from history or the news, ask: *Who benefits from this story being true?* Markets are rarely as irrational as the popular narrative suggests; usually, the "irrationality" is simply a failure of the observer to understand the underlying incentives.
